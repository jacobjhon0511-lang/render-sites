Black Wood Tea Ingredients: Eight Herbs and One Amino Acid

What each named ingredient is, and the main caution for each.

The seller names eight ingredients: horny goat weed, ginseng, maca root, tongkat ali, ginkgo biloba, tribulus terrestris, catuaba bark and L-arginine, with no amounts.* The seller's benefits text also mentions yohimbe, rhodiola, bacopa and beetroot, which are not on its list, so confirm the real formula on your pouch. Each note covers what the item is, what research exists and who should ask first, and the home page has a tap-through version.

Horny Goat Weed in Black Wood Tea

In short: Traditional vitality herb, named first.*

Horny goat weed (Epimedium) is a leafy herb long used in traditional Chinese practice, and its best-known compound is icariin. The seller names it first and calls it a cornerstone of the blend.

More on horny goat weed · safety and interactions

Ginseng in Black Wood Tea

In short: A well-known adaptogenic root.*

Panax ginseng is a famous root in traditional medicine, taken for energy and resilience. The seller lists it second.

More on ginseng · safety and interactions

Maca Root in Black Wood Tea

In short: Peruvian root, often called Peruvian ginseng.*

Maca is a radish-family root from the Andes, eaten as food and sold as a powder. The seller lists it third as a root that supports energy and stamina.

More on maca root · safety and interactions

Tongkat Ali in Black Wood Tea

In short: A Southeast Asian root used for vitality.*

Tongkat ali is a shrub root from Southeast Asia traditionally used for vitality, and some small studies have looked at its effect on hormone levels in men.

More on tongkat ali · safety and interactions

Ginkgo Biloba in Black Wood Tea

In short: A leaf extract studied for circulation.*

Ginkgo biloba is a leaf extract studied for circulation and memory, and the seller includes it for blood-flow support.

More on ginkgo biloba · safety and interactions

Tribulus Terrestris in Black Wood Tea

In short: A spiny plant used in traditional practice.*

Tribulus is a small spiny plant traditionally used for vitality and sold in sports and men's health products. The seller says it supports testosterone.

More on tribulus terrestris · safety and interactions

Catuaba Bark in Black Wood Tea

In short: A traditional Brazilian bark.*

Catuaba bark is a traditional Brazilian remedy for energy and mood. The seller lists it seventh for vitality and relaxation.

More on catuaba bark · safety and interactions

L-Arginine in Black Wood Tea

In short: An amino acid that the body uses to make nitric oxide.*

L-arginine is an amino acid, and the body converts it into nitric oxide, a molecule that relaxes blood vessels. The seller lists it last and ties it to blood flow.
